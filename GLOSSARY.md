# Chess Anthology

The shared language for describing chess works, people and their contexts. Units, relations and clusters describe perspectives within the anthology rather than fixed classifications of things.

## Framing

**Context**:
The selected people, works, events, places, practices and circumstances through which something is understood.

**Unit**:
An identifiable subject treated as the focus of a particular view. A player, composition, relation or entire cluster may be treated as a unit.
_Avoid_: Atom, indivisible entity

**Relation**:
A meaningful connection between identifiable subjects, such as authorship, participation or an encounter. A relation can itself become the subject of another relation or view.

**Cluster**:
A grouping of units, relations, other clusters or a mixture of these. Clusters may overlap; membership does not imply a single hierarchy or a shared identity.
_Avoid_: Community when the grouping is merely editorial

**View**:
A chosen perspective that gives subjects their roles as units, relations or clusters. Changing the view does not change the identity of the subjects.

## Chess subjects

**Person**:
An individual associated with chess through one or more roles, including player, composer, solver, teacher, maker or editor.

**Player**:
A person participating in chess play. The same person may also be a composer or solver.

**Composer**:
A person who deliberately constructs a chess composition.

**Composition**:
An authored chess position with a stipulation and intended solution or thematic idea. It can be viewed as a work in its own right or as a connection between creator, solver and publishing context.
_Avoid_: Puzzle when attribution and composed origin matter

**Problem**:
A composition with a specified task, such as mate in a given number of moves.

**Study**:
An endgame composition conventionally asking one side to win or draw.

**Game**:
A single played encounter between sides under specified rules.
_Avoid_: Match when referring specifically to one game

**Match**:
An organized contest between players or teams, consisting of one or more games. It connects its participants and can be examined through its constituent games.

**Tournament**:
An organized competitive event grouping participants and games or matches under a common format.

**Collection**:
A curated grouping of works, people, relations or other groupings. A composition collection groups compositions and may also contextualize their creators and connections.

**Era**:
An overlapping historical grouping defined by an explicit temporal and contextual frame. Its boundaries need not be universal across places or practices.

**Community**:
A group of people connected by documented shared practice or interaction. An editorially defined cluster is not necessarily a community.

**Entry**:
A reader-facing account of one or more subjects and their context.

**Research trail**:
An unfinished line of inquiry presented with its evidence status and next research task.

**Lesson annotation**:
An authored text or interaction attached to a position reached in a work's move history. An annotation belongs to the interpretation rather than changing the source moves.

**Try**:
A reader's legal move outside the supported lesson line, kept visible for reflection and feedback. It is distinct from the recorded score and does not imply an objectively losing move.
