# Project charter — Chess Anthology

Status: **founding intent; open to revision based on working experience and participant feedback.**

## Mission

Create a beautiful, honest, playable library that makes the human history and artistic creativity of chess easy and rewarding to explore.

Chess is more than competitive optimization. It is an evolving cultural practice: people playing games in historical contexts, inventing ideas, composing deliberate problems, writing about them, and sharing them with new generations. The application should help people encounter those works in ways a static book or passive video cannot.

## Who receives value

- **Curious readers and beginners:** A welcoming way into chess without needing an Elo-oriented curriculum.
- **Existing chess enthusiasts:** Fresh encounters with historical context, compositions, and overlooked connections.
- **Composers and historical creators:** Attribution and respectful presentation of deliberate artistic work.
- **Researchers, historians, teachers, and editors:** A medium for source-backed, inspectable interactive interpretation.
- **Contributors and maintainers:** A practical, open creative project whose work can be reused and whose decisions are understandable.
- **The wider chess commons:** A useful public artifact, not a closed repository of attention.

## Value proposition

**Discover → play → understand → explore.**

Select a person, era, event, game, study, or composition. Encounter a historically contextualized chess artifact. Interact with the board and ask for optional guidance. Read or listen to an original interpretation. Follow relevant connections without being pushed through a compulsory funnel.

### Why two products?

**Chess Through History** interprets *games that happened*, often embedded in multi-game events, politics and wider cultural change. The historical record and move order are central.

**The Art of Chess Composition** interprets *works deliberately constructed*. The author's intended stipulation, solution, themes, originality, and formal soundness are central. A composition must not be mislabeled as an actual game.

Both embody the same mission; neither should be subordinated to the other.

## Values and design tensions

| Value | Trade-off / commitment |
| --- | --- |
| Curiosity and autonomy | Exploration beats compulsory progression |
| Craft and restraint | One memorable lesson beats a hundred generic ones |
| Historical integrity | Source uncertainty is shown, not hidden for a better story |
| Human creation and credit | AI-assisted drafts never erase composers or authors |
| Openness and portability | A user can take their own content and records elsewhere |
| Accessibility | Visual beauty does not depend on audio, animation, or perfect motor control |
| Maintainability | A contributor-friendly codebase is part of the community benefit |
| Sustainability | Keep maintenance and financial burdens within real capacity |

## Non-goals for the initial product

Competitive matchmaking, chess ratings, retention gamification, synthetic "epic" narration, generic engine-generated tactic feeds, personalized advertising, follower mechanics, a full AI coaching service, bulk scraping at the expense of source terms, and creating an enterprise organization before it is needed.

## Meaningful evidence of success

Prefer *qualitative and task-level signals* over addictive engagement metrics:
- Can a newcomer open and meaningfully explore an artifact without instruction?
- Can they articulate why the chess idea or historical setting is interesting?
- Does the creator voluntarily reopen the app without an assigned exercise?
- Can a reader distinguish source-backed claims from interpretive commentary?
- Can another contributor author/import an artifact and preserve its credit?
- Can maintainers change or extend the app without substantial friction?

Do **not** use time-on-site, streaks, daily active users, or conversion funnels as the controlling design objective.

## Economic position

The project is a public-good-oriented craft undertaking with an **open startup hypothesis**, not a pre-committed company. Any future business model must support the tool's intrinsic value and the community's agency without making the app an engagement machine. No revenue model is proposed at this stage.

## Core product hypothesis

A small number of unusually well-crafted, historically grounded interactive chess artifacts can be more compelling and educational than either a passive video or a large undifferentiated puzzle collection. This is a hypothesis: build, observe, revise.
