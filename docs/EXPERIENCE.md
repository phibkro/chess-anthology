# Experience, design and editorial principles

## North star

**A delightful chess library you would open without an obligation to study.**

Target the level of polish, clarity and playful exploration admired in [Vimjoyer's Nixmap](https://vimjoyer.com/nixmap/) while developing our own visual identity and original content. Nixmap is an interaction-quality reference, not a design to copy.

## Interaction contract

1. **Immediate artifact:** A reader can open a game or composition without signup or onboarding.
2. **Board-first legibility:** The chess position is always readable; annotations never obscure essential squares.
3. **Optional prediction:** Pause before meaningful moves, not every move. Let readers play the historical continuation or try another legal line.
4. **Progressive disclosure:** Hint 1 directs attention; later hints identify the idea; reveal explains. A wrong move receives informative feedback without punishment.
5. **Nonlinear reading:** Back, forward, rewind, inspect variations and leave the story. Free exploration is not a failure state.
6. **Narrative integrity:** Explain source-backed facts separately from speculation and from modern chess analysis.
7. **Media with purpose:** Historical imagery, audio and motion aid orientation, atmosphere and understanding; never serve as generic spectacle.
8. **Quiet by default:** Fully useful without narration, autoplay, music or animations. Respect reduced-motion, contrast, keyboard and touch needs.
9. **Human editorial voice:** Clear, specific, restrained and curious. Avoid sensationalized, engagement-driven AI prose.
10. **Visible credit and corrections:** Composers, players, translators, editors and archive sources are part of the work.

## UX modes

- **Read:** Historical story with synchronized board moves.
- **Explore:** Free board and variations, with historical move distinguished from hypothetical continuation.
- **Solve:** Composition's stipulated challenge, with hints and precise solution feedback.
- **Inspect:** Sources, dates, attribution status, and notes on disagreement.
- **Import:** Local artifact first, followed by optional assisted enrichment. Imported/unreviewed must be plainly labeled.

Don't build all modes at once. Build the minimum that allows one complete composition and one complete historic-game narrative.

## Art direction exploration brief

Investigate: literary editorial typography; archival margin notes; authentic period imagery with credits; restrained chessboard colors; subtle piece motion; deliberate hierarchy and negative space. Avoid: giant gamification badges, generic AI portraits, faux historical imagery presented as authentic, dark patterns, overstuffed dashboards, and mandatory voice acting.

Create at least two visual directions before choosing a design system; evaluate against actual playable content, not decorative landing pages.

## Quality review questions

- Does the experience make the chess idea clearer?
- Would a chess-curious reader understand the distinction between history and composition?
- Is the story more accurate and compelling *because* the board is interactive?
- Can a reader reach sources, exercise agency, and escape a mistaken route?
- Are animations, labels, keyboard navigation, input latency and media quality good enough to merit attention?
- Can a reader enjoy it with sound off?

Quality is multi-dimensional: test chess correctness, history, accessibility, editorial value, and interface coherence separately.
