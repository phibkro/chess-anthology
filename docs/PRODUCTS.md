# Product concepts — two editorial products

Both products serve the Chess Anthology mission. They are **distinct editorial experiences** with possible shared software, not two separate startup businesses or necessarily two deployable apps.

## Product 1: Chess Through History

**Promise:** Explore chess history through games that were actually played, with their people, time, motivations and cultural setting made tangible.

**Primary object:** A recorded game, optionally situated within a match, tournament, simul, rivalry, tour, or chronological/curatorial series.

**Reader journey:** Choose a question, player, event or era → encounter context → replay a real game → pause at a meaningful decision → investigate alternatives → discover what happened and how it relates to the bigger story.

**Editorial examples (not yet sourced or implemented):**
- Paul Morphy's 1858 Opera Game and his match with Adolf Anderssen.
- Fischer–Spassky 1972 in Cold War and chess-cultural context.
- Kasparov–Deep Blue 1997 and later AlphaZero demonstrations, with original source material.
- Simultaneous exhibitions, where distinct games have separate records; move-order reconstruction must be labeled if unsupported.

**Must preserve:** Actual legal game record; provenance of date/place/players; contested claims; difference between played moves and hypothetical variations.

**Not:** An autoplay historical video or a drama generator. The board is an artifact to reason with, not just moving scenery.

## Product 2: The Art of Chess Composition

**Promise:** Encounter chess as deliberate human artistic construction, not merely a tactic generated by an engine.

**Primary object:** A composed chess problem or endgame study: author(s), position, stipulation, intended solution, and original source/version where known.

**Reader journey:** Discover a composer or artistic device → view stipulation and position → try a solution → ask for graded hints → examine defenses, alternatives and intended theme → read about the creator and publication.

**Editorial examples (subject to individual provenance):**
- A mate-in-two attributed to Morphy, published in 1856 (record disputed attribution rather than affirming authorship).
- Sam Loyd's *Excelsior*.
- Selected studies by Troitsky or Réti, or works from recognized composition archives.

**Must preserve:** Composer credit, origin, stipulation and rules variant, intended versus computer-found solutions, uncertainty, and any known unsoundness. See the [WFCC Codex](https://www.wfcc.ch/rules/codex/) for precise concepts such as cook, dual and short solution.

**Not:** A feed of engine-mined game positions mislabeled as composition.

## Shared reader capabilities — possible, not mandated

Playable board; rewind and branches; narration optional; responsive typography; hint layering; content metadata; source footnotes; portable import/export; optional researcher/librarian agent. The lead should extract shared components only when the first two vertical slices demonstrate actual shared needs.

## Experience-level differences

| Dimension | History | Composition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reader asks | "What happened, why, and in what context?" | "What has the composer constructed, and why is it elegant?" |
| Source of truth | Recorded game and historical evidence | Stipulation, author's intended solution, publication and composition conventions |
| Challenge | Predict a meaningful move in a *real game* | Find the key/solution for a *designed work* |
| Common trap | Fictionalizing a game for dramatic effect | Treating the work as an ordinary tactical shot |
| Natural grouping | Match, event, rival, tour, era | Composer, school, theme, source collection, year |
| Next exploration | Related games, people, politics, events | Other works, themes, compositional mechanisms |

## Testable product hypotheses

- **H1:** Contextualized move prediction makes a historic game more memorable than passive replay.
- **H2:** Knowing the author and artistic mechanism increases appreciation of a composed problem.
- **H3:** Voluntary lateral exploration is more valuable for this audience than a compulsory curriculum.
- **H4:** One accessible reader can support both without flattening their editorial character.
- **H5:** An inspectable import flow makes the product useful before an extensive curated collection exists.

These require observation; they are not established market facts.
